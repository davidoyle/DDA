## Fiscal impact and growth modelling

### Know what growth will require before the target is adopted

DDA models the chain from population and households to housing, employment, land, servicing, and public revenue so growth choices can be compared against their consequences.

### The decision this supports

A population target is only the first input in a growth decision. The target also depends on households, housing supply, employment, land, infrastructure, operating cost, and the public revenue that follows.

DDA makes that chain visible. Staff and council can compare scenarios, see which assumption changes the result, and identify the constraint that arrives first.

### What you receive

- A deterministic growth-chain model.
- Population, household, and housing-demand projections.
- Employment and land-demand analysis.
- Infrastructure and servicing constraint review.
- Fiscal-impact or public-revenue scenarios where the evidence supports them.
- Scenario comparison tables.
- A source and assumption register.
- A council or board decision brief.
- A technical appendix with calculations and limitations.

### How DDA works

The model begins with a defined decision unit and named source data. Every input is classified as ACTUAL, PROXY, or FLAG.

ACTUAL is verified information from a named primary source. PROXY is a derived value or comparison with its basis shown. FLAG is a known gap that could change the result.

The model does not turn a forecast into a promise. It shows the conditions behind the choice.

### When this is useful

Bring DDA in when a growth target is being updated, housing need has been calculated without a land or servicing test, or council needs to compare the consequences of different growth paths.

The work also fits an OCP or regional plan that needs a defensible land-demand basis, and a public hearing where the assumptions will be tested.

## Official Community Plan review and policy analysis

### Give the plan a record it can defend

DDA tests plans, bylaws, needs reports, and stated objectives against delivery evidence, statutory requirements, and observable conditions.

### The decision this supports

An OCP can be internally consistent and still be difficult to deliver. Housing targets may exceed construction economics. Land-use policy may overlook servicing. A bylaw may carry forward language that no longer matches the community or the law.

DDA gives staff and council a clear account of what the plan says, what the evidence supports, and which choices remain open.

### What you receive

- OCP evidence and policy review.
- Housing, land-use, and growth findings.
- OCP to zoning crosswalk.
- Zoning bylaw housekeeping and modernization review.
- Development Permit Area and hazard-policy review where required.
- Statutory and policy alignment matrix.
- Policy options with stated trade-offs.
- Engagement and public-hearing decision material.
- Annotated source and gap register.

### How DDA works

DDA reads the plan with the records around it: census data, housing needs, budgets, servicing studies, development activity, prior policies, and public decisions.

Each policy is tested for what it changes, who must deliver it, and what the evidence says about feasibility. Confirmed information is separated from comparison data and unresolved local questions.

### When this is useful

Bring DDA in for a comprehensive OCP review, zoning bylaw housekeeping, a housing-target delivery test, policy choices before a public hearing, or an independent review before adoption.

### The decision this supports

An economic development strategy can describe sectors and opportunities without showing what has to be true for them to become projects, jobs, investment, or revenue.

## Labour market analysis

### Find the constraint inside the workforce number

DDA builds labour-market evidence that shows who is available, what demand requires, where the pipeline breaks, and which local conditions change the result.

### The decision this supports

A headline shortage can point to several different problems: participation, training capacity, occupational mismatch, housing, childcare, health, wages, or timing. Each one calls for a different response.

DDA separates those problems before action is chosen. The work helps a municipality, employer group, project owner, or training partner see which lever reaches the binding constraint.

### What you receive

- A seven-domain labour-market baseline.
- Employment, occupation, demographic, and participation analysis.
- Labour demand by project phase or sector.
- Skills and training-pipeline assessment.
- Indigenous participation and local-context analysis where required.
- Housing and other enabling-condition review.
- Supply and demand scenarios.
- Employer, educator, or council summaries.
- Evidence register with gaps and resolution paths.
- Implementation and monitoring framework.

### How DDA works

DDA starts with the decision unit. A total workforce figure is not enough when one trade, credential, or date controls the outcome.

Sources are classified before the model is built. Categories remain separate when combining them would count the same people or costs twice. Ranges are used where the evidence supports a range. Unresolved gaps remain visible.

### When this is useful

Bring DDA in for a labour-market plan, a project workforce assessment, a training-capacity review, or a municipal analysis that needs to connect housing and labour supply.

The work also fits a grant or regional strategy that needs measurable labour outcomes and a clear account of what the data can establish.

## Resource-sector and complex planning analysis

### See what controls the project before the schedule does

DDA connects workforce, logistics, infrastructure, regulation, housing, Indigenous participation, and project timing to show where a complex plan is exposed.

### The decision this supports

Large projects are often described through capital value, jobs, or approval stage. Delivery depends on a smaller set of constraints that may sit across different agencies and timelines.

DDA maps the system around the project and identifies the party that controls the next decisive point. That gives operators, contractors, communities, public agencies, and advisors a common record for the issue in front of them.

### What you receive

- Project and regional evidence register.
- Workforce demand by trade and phase.
- Concurrent-project and mobility analysis.
- Training, housing, infrastructure, and logistics constraints.
- Regulatory and approval sequence.
- Actor and decision-control map.
- Schedule and delay-exposure scenarios.
- Audience-specific briefing material.
- Risk register with evidence status and resolution paths.

### How DDA works

The work begins with the date that cannot move and works backward through the conditions required to meet it. DDA disaggregates the headline risk, tests substitution and timing assumptions, and shows where the evidence is strong or incomplete.

The analysis can be written for a project owner, contractor, union, Nation, investor, regulator, or public body. The question and the buyer determine which side of the constraint matters.

### When this is useful

Bring DDA in when a project has a workforce or commissioning risk, several projects draw from the same regional labour or logistics pool, or a schedule risk has appeared before the formal schedule reflects it.

The work also fits a public agency that needs to understand local project conditions, or a contractor or advisor that needs a fast evidence base for a live engagement.

## Long-range financial and scenario planning

### See how the assumptions change the result

DDA builds financial models that connect project economics, public revenue, costs, and policy choices across a defined planning horizon.

### The decision this supports

Long-range models can create false certainty when the result hides the assumptions that drive it. Decision-makers need to know which inputs change the answer, how far the answer moves, and where the evidence stops.

DDA gives each scenario a visible basis. The model can support fiscal planning, project review, royalty and tax analysis, public investment, or a comparison between jurisdictions.

### What you receive

- Model architecture and source register.
- Base, upside, downside, or policy scenarios.
- Revenue, cost, tax, royalty, or fiscal modules as required.
- Sensitivity and breakeven analysis.
- Range and assumption tables.
- Project and public-sector outputs where both are needed.
- Model documentation and handoff material.
- Executive or council briefing.
- Technical appendix and limitations register.

### How DDA works

Official figures stay visible. Derived values show their basis. The model distinguishes measured inputs, comparable proxies, and variables that require client or institutional data.

A useful model lets the reader see what would have to change for the decision to change.

### When this is useful

Bring DDA in when a long-range plan needs scenario testing, a fiscal estimate depends on linked assumptions, a public body needs a transparent revenue model, or a board or council needs to understand downside exposure.

The work also fits an existing model that is difficult to audit or explain.

## Public-interest research and evidence packages

### Build the record before the argument

DDA turns a difficult public question into a sourced evidence package for oversight, journalism, legal analysis, advocacy, or institutional review.

### The decision this supports

Public-interest questions often involve records produced by different institutions, under different mandates, over several years. A single document rarely contains the finding.

DDA assembles the record, tests competing explanations, identifies what cannot be verified, and writes the result for the audience that has to act on it.

### What you receive

- Research question and scope memo.
- Primary-source evidence register.
- ACTUAL, PROXY, and FLAG classification.
- Chronology, actor map, or process map where useful.
- Structural gap or cost analysis.
- Hypothesis and falsification framework.
- Briefing memo or publication-ready report.
- Verification appendix.
- Plain-language or executive version.
- Source, limitation, and correction log.

### How DDA works

DDA reads public records as a system. It compares what an institution says with what its records show while keeping the boundary between non-verifiable and false claims intact.

The work is written so another reader can retrace the finding. If a number cannot be established, the package says what would be needed to establish it.

### When this is useful

Bring DDA in when a public claim needs to be tested against the record, a legal or policy team needs a documentary base, an oversight body needs a chronology, or a journalist needs a publication-ready evidence package.

It also fits a public organization that needs to understand a system failure before choosing a response.

## Insight: When a housing target outruns delivery

### The target is only one part of the question

A municipality can calculate how many homes it needs and still lack a defensible account of how those homes will be delivered.

The gap appears when the target is read beside the records that control delivery: construction cost, builder response, land, servicing, financing, and past production.

In one small-community record, the stated need was 291 units over twenty years. Historical delivery was 3.6 units a year. The community’s own 2024 duplex procurement also recorded that builders could not deliver at the stated $300,000 to $320,000 range.

Those facts do not answer the policy question by themselves. They do define it. A target that ignores delivery conditions is a choice about risk, not a forecast of output.

### What the record changes

The analysis connects the target to the conditions required to reach it. It asks which part of the chain is binding, which assumption can be changed, and which missing fact needs to be resolved before adoption.

Vacancy data can add another part of the picture. In the Trail, Warfield, and Fruitvale area, the recorded vacancy rate fell from 12 percent to 3 percent between 2015 and 2017. Demand was present while delivery did not respond at the same pace.

### The decision

Staff and council can choose a target with a clear account of what it requires. They can also identify the policy, land, servicing, or delivery action that needs to change if the target is to become more than a number.

### Sourced basis

The analysis used the Village of Fruitvale OCP and zoning work, the Village’s 2024 duplex RFP, housing-need material, historical delivery data, and vacancy information for the surrounding area.

## Insight: The trade gap hidden inside a workforce number

### A shortage is not one problem

A project can report a large workforce requirement and still leave the decisive question unanswered: which occupation, at what point in the schedule, has no practical substitute?

DDA’s resource-sector work separates demand by trade, project phase, geography, training pipeline, housing, and competing projects. That changes the meaning of the headline number.

In the Eskay Creek analysis, the recorded gap was 67 percent for pipefitters. The issue was tied to a commissioning date and a regional labour pool shared with other projects. A general recruitment target would not resolve that constraint.

### What the record changes

The analysis identifies the party that controls the next point: a project owner, contractor, union, training provider, Nation, investor, or public agency. The useful question depends on who is making the decision and which side of the constraint they occupy.

### The decision

The project can test a specific intervention against a specific gap. It can also see whether a proposed solution reaches the trade and date that control the outcome.

### Sourced basis

The analysis used project labour requirements, trade-level supply and demand, regional project concurrency, training information, and housing and mobility conditions recorded in the Eskay Creek and Golden Triangle work.

## Insight: What a FLAG tells you

### Unknown is a status

A report becomes harder to defend when a missing number is hidden inside a confident sentence.

DDA uses FLAG for a known gap in the evidence. A FLAG records what is missing, why it matters, what would resolve it, and what changes if it remains unresolved.

The boundary matters. An unresolved figure does not prove that an institution is wrong. It shows what the available record cannot establish.

### What the register changes

ACTUAL identifies verified information from a named source. PROXY identifies a derived value or comparison and shows its basis. FLAG keeps a material unknown visible instead of allowing it to become an unexamined assumption.

That lets a decision-maker see where more data is worth obtaining and where the decision must proceed with a stated limit.

### The decision

The reader can distinguish a fact from an estimate and an unknown from a conclusion. The resulting decision may be more cautious, more targeted, or more confident, depending on what the record supports.

### Sourced basis

ACTUAL, PROXY, and FLAG are the evidence classifications used throughout DDA’s Data Lexicon and project registers.

## Selected work

### Questions answered in the record

Much of DDA’s work is confidential. These examples describe the question, the analysis, and the decision the work was built to support.

### A housing target that did not match delivery

A small community needed a housing target and an updated policy framework.

DDA connected the stated need to historical delivery, local housing evidence, builder economics, land, and servicing. The work exposed a gap between the target and the conditions required to deliver it.

Staff and council could see which parts of the target were supported, which required a policy choice, and which needed new evidence.

### A workforce gap tied to one trade and one date

A resource project had a large workforce requirement and a fixed commissioning window.

DDA disaggregated demand by trade and phase, then tested the regional training, housing, and competing-project conditions. The headline shortage became a specific trade gap with a schedule consequence.

The party responsible for the next constraint could be identified before the problem reached the formal schedule.

### A regional strategy built from evidence status

A regional body needed an economic-development and tourism strategy that could move into implementation.

DDA built an evidence register, classified sources, identified missing local information, and connected actions to owners and timing. The strategy separated what was known from what was assumed and what still needed to be found.

The board could prioritise actions against actual capacity and evidence.

### A financial number opened up

A public decision depended on a large official revenue or fiscal figure.

DDA decomposed the number, tested the statutory and behavioural assumptions, and modelled bounded scenarios. The official figure stayed visible while the uncertainty below it became usable.

Decision-makers could see which assumption changed the result and where more data was worth obtaining.

### Public reporting against observable behaviour

An institution reported progress against a public mandate or outcome.

DDA read mandates, budgets, appeals, enforcement records, communications, and implementation data together. The analysis measured the gap between what was announced and what the system produced.

Oversight readers could ask a specific accountability question tied to a source and date.

### A recovery plan tested against the physical system

A fire-affected community needed an economic-development path while rebuilding conditions remained uneven.

DDA examined businesses, housing, insurance, infrastructure, municipal assets, governance, and the planning record. Recovery was separated into conditions that could be acted on and conditions that required external resolution.

The community could direct limited capacity toward the constraint most likely to affect delivery.

### Evidence built under another consultant’s deadline

DDA gathered, classified, and structured the source material into a usable register. The lead consultant could move into analysis with the evidence status already visible.
