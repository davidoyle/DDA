# Institutional and policy analysis

Public claims and institutional questions often span mandates, budgets, decisions, communications, appeals, enforcement, and implementation. DDA reconstructs that record so a team can see what is supported, what conflicts, and what remains unresolved.

## When the record is fragmented

The work may begin with a public statement, policy proposal, institutional response, oversight question, or collection of records nobody has assembled. Repetition does not make a claim well supported. Several reports may trace back to one source, use different definitions, or leave the critical implementation step undocumented.

DDA builds the chronology, follows claims to primary sources, and maps the actors and decision points. Policy claims can then be compared with implementation evidence, budgets, outputs, and the mechanism expected to produce the stated result.

## Test what the evidence can support

A claim-and-source register records what each claim says, the source and date behind it, conflicting evidence, and the inference the material can responsibly carry. Competing explanations can be tested against observable evidence. Material gaps remain visible along with what depends on them and where resolution may be possible.

This approach supports policy, legal, media, advocacy, oversight, and public-sector teams without treating an allegation as established.

## Forms the work may take

A scoped assignment can produce a primary-source register, chronology, actor or process map, policy-mechanism analysis, hypothesis and falsification structure, briefing memo, publication-ready report, verification appendix, plain-language version, or correction log.

This is research and analysis. It is not legal representation, privileged work, medical advice, investment advice, or a finding that an allegation is true. Sensitive personal, Indigenous, confidential, or commercially restricted material requires an agreed scope, authorization, and handling method.
