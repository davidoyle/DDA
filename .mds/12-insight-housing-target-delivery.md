# When a housing target outruns delivery

**Perspective · Growth and land use · 5 minute read**

## Need is a number. Delivery is a chain.

A housing target can be well calculated and still leave the central question unanswered: what has to be true for anyone to deliver the units?

The chain runs from need to households, land, policy, servicing, construction economics, financing, approvals, and actual production. If one link is assumed, a target can become a promise before its binding constraint is visible.

## The gap between need and delivery

In one small-community housing review, the stated need was 291 units over twenty years. Historical delivery was 3.6 units a year. A 2024 duplex procurement found that builders could not deliver at the stated $300,000 to $320,000 range.

Those figures occupy different parts of the same system. The target describes need. The production rate shows what had been delivered. The procurement result tests whether the local market could produce the proposed form at the stated price. Read together, they expose the distance between a target and the mechanism expected to deliver it.

The review also cited a fall in the vacancy rate from 12 percent to 3 percent between 2015 and 2017 in the Trail, Warfield, and Fruitvale area. That raised a practical question: was demand increasing while the mechanism that produces supply remained constrained?

## Trace the target to delivery

| Link | Question for the decision |
| --- | --- |
| Target | What need, population, or household basis is being used, and when was it measured? |
| Land | Which capacity is theoretical, realistically serviceable, permitted, and policy-adjusted? |
| Policy | What OCP, zoning, financing, or program mechanism is expected to produce units? |
| Servicing | What water, sewer, roads, approvals, or operating condition must hold? |
| Construction | Can the local delivery market meet the cost, form, and timing assumption? |
| Delivery | What do production and approvals show has happened? |
| Implication | Which condition binds first, and which choice remains with council? |

This trace separates the policy objective from the empirical finding. It also shows which conditions sit within municipal control and which depend on the market, infrastructure, financing, or another actor.

## What changes for the decision-maker

Staff can attach a delivery chain to the target. Council can revise the target, change the delivery mechanism, commission a missing land or servicing input, or proceed with the exposure clearly stated. Builders and advisers can see which assumption needs a market answer instead of another policy sentence.

The useful output may be a capacity reconciliation, evidence register, and decision brief that puts the target, source status, constraint, owner, date, and next question in one place.

## Practical implications

- Pair each housing target with land, servicing, construction, and financing conditions.
- Keep theoretical capacity separate from realistic and policy-adjusted capacity.
- Put historical delivery beside the target, with its date and definition.
- Test whether the proposed policy reaches the constraint.
- Assign an owner and review date to each material gap.
- Refresh local figures before using them in a current decision.

**Source note:** Historical figures refer to Village of Fruitvale housing and planning materials, including a 2024 duplex procurement.
