# What we do

DDA enters complex problems before every part of the question is settled. We learn the system, reconstruct the evidence, find what is materially shaping the outcome, and establish what that finding changes.

The work may begin with a stalled project, a new mandate, an RFP, an unsupported number, a strategy that is not moving, scattered public records, or a concern that something important has been missed.

[Talk to DDA](./16-contact.md)

## How DDA gets into a problem

### Start where the problem is

We begin with the material already available and the outcome someone is trying to reach. A finished scope is not required. Early work clarifies the question, the decision context, the people and institutions involved, and the fixed dates or commitments.

### Reconstruct the system

We assemble sources, definitions, dates, datasets, actors, dependencies, and prior claims. This can cross economics, policy, infrastructure, labour, land, institutions, finance, and project timing when the problem requires it.

### Find what matters and test the consequence

We look for the constraint, gap, dependency, assumption, or exposure that carries the outcome. Then we test what it changes in cost, capacity, timing, feasibility, risk, or the strength of a public claim.

### Build what the work requires

The finding determines the output. It may call for a model, evidence base, GIS analysis, chronology, scenario set, strategy, register, brief, implementation sequence, or decision tool.
