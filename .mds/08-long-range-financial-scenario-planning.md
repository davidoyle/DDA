# Long-range financial and scenario planning

## Which assumption would change the decision?

A fiscal, tax, royalty, investment, capital, or long-range model can produce a precise answer without showing why the answer moves. DDA opens the model so the decision-maker can see the assumptions, timing, and evidence beneath it.

## The consequential decision

A board, finance team, project sponsor, ministry, or treasury lead may need to approve a direction, update an input, compare policy choices, accept a range, or defer a decision until a material assumption is resolved.

## The controlling condition

The answer may depend on timing, behaviour, source vintage, legal treatment, pass-through, cost, or one missing data point. The controlling condition is the assumption that changes the choice or materially changes the range.

## The analysis

DDA treats scenarios as controlled tests. The assumptions register pairs each material input with its source or status, horizon, sensitivity, and decision effect. The model then compares a base case with upside, downside, or policy-adjusted cases.

The analysis can combine defined planning horizons and deterministic modelling with bottom-up fiscal analysis, pass-through assumptions, reconciliation, and business-impact framing. Current laws, rates, and figures are checked for the decision at hand.

## The usable output

Depending on scope, the work can include model architecture, a source register, base and alternative scenarios, revenue and cost modules, sensitivity and breakeven tables, range analysis, an assumptions register, model documentation, technical appendix, executive brief, and handoff notes.

The brief states the choice, the evidence, the assumption carrying the result, the trade-off, and the open condition. It does not convert a model into specialist legal, tax, engineering, investment, or securities advice.
