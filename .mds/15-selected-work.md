# Selected work

## Decisions change when the controlling condition becomes visible

The work below spans communities and regions, major projects and workforce, and policy and institutions. In each case, DDA connected several systems, found the condition carrying the outcome, and built an instrument the decision-maker could use.

## Planning growth against real capacity

**Decision:** How should a city plan for twenty years of population, housing, employment, and land demand without overstating what its growth areas could carry?

**Controlling condition:** The headline requirement depended on the difference between theoretical land capacity and feasible capacity after geography, infrastructure, development pipeline, and readiness were applied.

**Analysis:** DDA built a deterministic demand model and a separate allocation and feasibility model, then tested multiple growth paths against spatial plans and capacity constraints.

**Shift:** The question moved from choosing a single forecast to comparing where growth could fit, where pressure accumulated, and which assumption changed the choice.

**Instrument:** Municipal growth model, scenario matrix, capacity reconciliation, data lexicon, and decision brief.

## Turning employment land into an executable local strategy

**Decision:** Which land and economic actions could support more local employment in a community where many residents travelled elsewhere for work?

**Controlling condition:** The nominal employment-land inventory narrowed sharply after parcel size, slope, servicing, environmental constraints, and realistic development readiness were considered.

**Analysis:** DDA connected parcel-level spatial analysis with workforce characteristics, commuting patterns, sector fit, servicing, market demand, and engagement requirements.

**Shift:** The question moved from how much employment land existed to which sites and sectors could move first, what each required, and where local action had leverage.

**Instrument:** Working land inventory, editable spatial data, demand and absorption scenarios, readiness screen, evidence register, and implementation roadmap.

## Finding the workforce constraint inside a major-project schedule

**Decision:** What could protect a fixed construction and commissioning window when several projects were competing for the same regional workforce?

**Controlling condition:** Aggregate labour supply concealed the specific trades, qualifications, rotations, accommodation, logistics, and dates that controlled critical phases.

**Analysis:** DDA decomposed demand by trade and phase, tested the training pipeline and usable regional supply, mapped concurrent-project demand, and modelled timing and delay exposure.

**Shift:** The issue moved from general recruitment to the levers that could reach the constraint: procurement timing, work packaging, fabrication, mobility, accommodation, and sequence.

**Instrument:** Trade-by-phase model, contested-labour map, timing scenarios, sensitivity analysis, mitigation sequence, and executive brief.

## Building a regional strategy around implementation capacity

**Decision:** Which economic-development actions should a region prioritize when mining expansion, forestry contraction, housing pressure, tourism, and demographic change were moving at the same time?

**Controlling condition:** The region had assets and opportunities, but execution depended on staff capacity, current data, accountable ownership, infrastructure, and decisions controlled by other organizations.

**Analysis:** DDA built a regional evidence base, mapped system feedback and delays, tested opportunities against local control and dependencies, and identified the information and governance gaps affecting several actions at once.

**Shift:** The strategy moved from a list of opportunities to a sequence: what could move now, what required another actor, which missing input deserved priority, and what should trigger a change in direction.

**Instrument:** Strategic diagnosis, evidence and gap register, leverage analysis, owner-and-dependency matrix, implementation framework, monitoring measures, and adaptation triggers.

## Opening a fiscal policy number into business exposure

**Decision:** How should organizations understand the effect of a tax change when the headline estimate did not show how costs would move through different firms and sectors?

**Controlling condition:** Exposure depended on taxable share, service mix, pass-through, firm size, compliance cost, margin structure, and behavioural response.

**Analysis:** DDA constructed the taxable base from the bottom up, tested multiple incidence scenarios, compared sector and firm profiles, and traced the assumptions carrying the result.

**Shift:** A single policy number became a range of operating exposures, with the decisive assumptions and practical thresholds visible.

**Instrument:** Fiscal-impact model, assumptions register, scenario tables, firm-level calculator, risk flags, and decision brief.
