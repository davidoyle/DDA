# The trade gap hidden inside a workforce number

**Perspective · Labour and resource projects · 5 minute read**

## A workforce total cannot staff a date

A project may have a large workforce requirement and a fixed construction or commissioning phase. The useful question is narrower: which occupation, qualification, location, and date has no practical substitute?

That is the decision unit. Without it, recruitment, training, housing, mobility, and procurement measures can be aimed at the wrong shortage.

## The trade inside the headline

In one resource-project analysis, demand was separated across millwrights, construction electricians, heavy-duty mechanics, and pipefitters. The analysis then tested the training pipeline, geographic availability, competing projects, and timing.

That work isolated a substantial pipefitter gap associated with a commissioning window. A regional pressure map connected workforce demand to logistics, roads, power, regulation, and concurrent projects.

The useful move is the decomposition. A broad workforce number becomes actionable when it is tied to the trade and date that control the phase.

## Decompose the headline

| Decision unit | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Occupation | Which work cannot be covered by a different trade? |
| Qualification | How much of the stated supply is certified or deployable? |
| Place | Can the available worker reach and remain at the project? |
| Timing | Does the pipeline arrive before the required phase? |
| Competition | Which other projects draw from the same regional pool? |
| Demand | What does the phase require, and when? |

A registration count is not effective supply. A worker with the right occupation may be unavailable in the required region. A worker available now may be committed during the peak phase. A training program may help later and still miss the commissioning date.

## Test the proposed response

DDA tests each response against the binding constraint. A general recruitment target may miss a certified-trade gap. A training program that produces workers later may not change the phase. If accommodation or mobility is the constraint, training alone will not solve it.

The output can be a trade-by-phase workforce table, evidence-status register, dependency map, and decision brief that assigns the next data request or action to an owner.

## What changes for the decision-maker

The project team can decide whether to change procurement, training, mobility, accommodation, sequencing, or the phase assumption. A workforce lead can set a target against usable supply. A public agency can distinguish a general labour-market condition from a project-specific constraint.

## Practical implications

- Break the headline into occupation, qualification, place, timing, and demand.
- Separate registrations from completion and deployability.
- Test competing-project demand before assuming a regional pool is available.
- Link each intervention to the constraint it can reach.
- Record the date after which an option may no longer be available.
- Keep sensitive project and Nation-specific information outside public materials unless it is cleared.

**Source note:** The example draws on Eskay Creek workforce analysis and Golden Triangle regional planning material current at the time of analysis.
