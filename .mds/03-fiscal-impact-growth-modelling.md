# Fiscal impact and growth modelling

A growth target or fiscal forecast can look settled while its result depends on land availability, servicing, market absorption, operating costs, timing, or the definition of the source number. DDA opens the headline and rebuilds the chain beneath it.

## When the numbers need to be opened

The question may arrive as a population target, land-demand estimate, infrastructure request, revenue forecast, or long-range plan. These figures often combine different units, dates, and assumptions. The first task is to establish what each number measures and how it connects to the decision.

## Capacity on paper and capacity that can be delivered

Theoretical land capacity is different from realistic, serviceable, and policy-adjusted capacity. Housing need is different from construction output. Revenue growth is different from the net fiscal position after capital and operating costs.

DDA connects population, households, employment, parcels, infrastructure, costs, revenues, and time. This shows where a target meets a delivery limit and which assumption is carrying the financial result.

## What gets tested

The analysis can reconcile capacity across scenarios, document the source and vintage of each material input, and test sensitivities around servicing, absorption, cost, timing, and policy. A base case sits beside credible alternatives so the buyer can see how far the answer moves and why.

## What the work may produce

Outputs can include a growth model, parcel or capacity reconciliation, fiscal forecast, scenario matrix, assumptions register, sensitivity table, technical appendix, and concise decision brief. The form depends on the question and the people who need to use the result.

Current law, tax treatment, engineering conditions, and client data require current or specialist review. DDA does not provide tax, legal, engineering, investment, or securities advice.
